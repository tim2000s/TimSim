# Proximity to real closed-loop cohorts under the simulator as configured, against stock UVA/Padova S2008

## Introduction

The UVA/Padova S2008 model reproduces the glucoregulatory physiology of type 1 diabetes and omits
most of what surrounds it. It has no infusion set that stops absorbing, no sensor error beyond
quantisation, no person who mistimes a meal or misjudges its carbohydrate, and no day-to-day
movement in insulin sensitivity. Its subjects are also dosed from settings derived from their own
physiology, a quantity no person has. A cohort run under a closed loop on that basis spends more
time in the target range than the people such a loop is prescribed to.

This package changes both parts. It adds four layers to the simulated person, covering
physiological variation, behaviour, sensor error and infusion site failure, and it titrates each
subject's settings until that subject's own record clears the consensus hypoglycaemia thresholds.
What follows reports how far the configured simulator sits from three real closed-loop populations
compared with the stock model, at each of three meal-announcement conditions.

## Methods

The population holds 600 subjects generated by resampling the thirty published UVA/Padova subjects
within age class, of which 330 have been through titration. Cohorts were drawn from it to match
each real reference on age and recorded total daily insulin, giving 84 subjects against the
closed-loop arm of DCLP3, 64 against the OpenAPS Data Commons and 73 against CLOSE-IT. Each cohort
was run under the algorithm its reference population used, a Control-IQ stand-in for DCLP3 and
oref0 SMB for the other two, so a simulated cohort is never compared with participants using a
different system.

Four arms were run per pairing, 28 days each, 884 runs in total. The stock arm is the model as
simglucose supplies it: no layers, and each subject dosed from the settings its own physiology
implies, with the algorithm told the true carbohydrate content of every meal at the minute it is
eaten. The remaining three carry all four layers on titrated settings and differ in what the
algorithm is told about meals. Announced declares every meal, default declares 80 per cent of main
meals and 35 per cent of snacks, and unannounced declares none. In all three the declared amount
carries a 25 per cent coefficient of variation against the amount eaten, and the carbohydrate
reaches the gut ten minutes after the person is recorded as eating, because the estimating and the
gastric delay are both part of what the layers add.

Sensor noise is drawn per subject and held for the run, from a lognormal with a log standard
deviation of 0.33 about a jitter of 1.93 mg/dL. Real users differ from one another in how noisy
their sensor traces are, and a single noise level applied to every subject removes a source of
between-person spread that the reference populations carry.

Outcomes are read from the sensor trace, since the real references are sensor traces. Time in range
is 70 to 180 mg/dL (3.9 to 10.0 mmol/L), time below range is reported at 70 mg/dL and at 54 mg/dL
(3.0 mmol/L), and time above range at 180 mg/dL. Each is given as a median with a 95 per cent
percentile interval resampled over subjects. Both the median and the mean are reported because the
trials report means and a third of subjects record no time below 54 mg/dL, where the two statistics
differ by a factor of three.

An arm is recorded as differing from its reference when the reference median falls outside the
arm's interval. A difference expressed in units of the between-person spread of the reference
describes its size and does not establish that it exceeds the variation between cohorts drawn from
the same population.

Completion is reported for every arm. A run terminates when glucose leaves the range over which the
model is defined, terminating runs are those that departed furthest from target, and every median
is therefore conditioned on survival.

DCLP3 supplies 67 participants with per-participant outcomes on all six measures and the Commons 77
users on the same six. CLOSE-IT publishes a mean and a standard deviation for time in range and no
participant-level data, so it supports no median, no spread and no test.

At the default announcement condition the configured simulator reduced the difference from the

mg/dL records 0.4 per cent under the stock model against a reference 0.2, which the test does not

| Mean glucose | 0.56 | 0.64 | 0.29 | 0.43 |

The population is narrower than either reference on the four measures describing where glucose
sits, carrying 0.43 to 0.70 of the real spread, and wider on both hypoglycaemia measures and on
variability. Configuring the simulator narrows the excess on hypoglycaemia and widens the deficit
on the location measures, so it moves the two groups of measures in opposite directions.

differently selected group than for the stock arm.

## Discussion

The configured simulator sits closer to all three real populations than the stock model does on
every measure describing where glucose sits. Against DCLP3 at the default announcement condition it
halves the difference in time in range and in mean glucose, and reduces the difference in
variability from 7.3 percentage points to 1.9. Against the Commons it reaches agreement on mean
glucose and on variability, which the stock model does not.

Agreement is not achieved against DCLP3 on any measure at the default condition except variability.
An algorithm benchmarked on this configuration is benchmarked on a population that remains about
ten percentage points of time in range better controlled than that trial arm, and the direction of
that residual should be assumed to flatter any algorithm scored on it.

Withdrawing meal announcement moves every measure closer to the DCLP3 participants and brings time
in range to within 5.8 points. This is a property of the benchmark rather than of the model. Real
participants announced their meals, so an unannounced arm reaches the trial's outcomes by imposing a
burden those people did not carry.

Hypoglycaemia is where the configuration is furthest from real people, and it moves away from them.
Under the stock model time below 54 mg/dL cannot be distinguished from the DCLP3 participants, and
under every configured arm it can, at 0.6 to 0.9 per cent against a reference 0.2. Its
between-subject spread is 5.87 times the reference, the largest excess in the spread table. It also
carried the widest variation between disjoint cohorts drawn from this population when that was last
measured, at 1.06 to 3.65 spreads depending on the cohort and algorithm, although that measurement
was made under a settings store two revisions old and has not been repeated against the present one.
Hypoglycaemia inferences from this bench are the least supported of any reported here.

Two features of the spread table bear on how a benchmark should be read. The population carries
about two thirds of the real between-subject variation in time in range and mean glucose, so a
cohort drawn from it will look more homogeneous than a trial population and an algorithm's worst
subjects will be less bad than the worst real users. Variability and both hypoglycaemia measures
run wider than real instead. Drawing a per-subject sensor noise level rather than sharing one
brought the variability spread from 1.80 to 1.37 of the reference on DCLP3 and to 1.14 on the
Commons, which is the closest either has been, and it did not move the location measures.

Three limitations bound these results. Completion differs between the stock and configured arms by
up to 16 subjects, so they do not describe the same set of people, and the configured medians are
conditioned on a group that excludes more of the subjects reaching a terminating low. The CLOSE-IT
reference is a published summary without participant-level data, so no test was possible against it
and its cohort was matched on drawn rather than recorded ages. The three reference populations also
differ in kind: DCLP3 randomised its participants, the Commons is self-selected among people who
chose an open-source system, and agreement with one is not agreement with the other.
