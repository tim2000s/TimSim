# The contribution of each simulation layer to agreement with real closed-loop cohorts

## Introduction

The UVA/Padova S2008 model reproduces the glucoregulatory physiology of type 1 diabetes and
omits most of what surrounds it. It has no infusion set that stops absorbing, no sensor error
beyond quantisation, no person who mistimes a meal or misjudges its carbohydrate, and no
day-to-day movement in insulin sensitivity. A cohort of its subjects run under a closed loop
therefore spends more time in the target range than the people such a loop is prescribed to.

This package adds four layers to close part of that distance. Their combined effect has been
reported before. Their individual contributions have not, and neither has the contribution of the
settings the subjects are dosed on, which is a choice this package makes rather than a property of
the model. The measurement below separates them, against three real populations and the algorithm
each of those populations used.

## Methods

The population holds 600 subjects generated by resampling the thirty published UVA/Padova
subjects within age class. Cohorts were drawn from it to match each real reference on age and
recorded total daily insulin, giving 84 subjects against the closed-loop arm of DCLP3, 63 against
the OpenAPS Data Commons and 71 against CLOSE-IT. Each cohort was run under the algorithm its
reference population used: a Control-IQ stand-in for DCLP3, oref0 SMB for the other two. A fourth
pairing of DCLP3 subjects under oref0 was excluded because no published cohort of those
participants running that algorithm exists.

Eleven arms were run for 28 days each, 2,398 runs in total. The first pair establish the
reference points. In the stock arm each subject is dosed from settings implied by its own
physiology, which is what the published parameter tables give and what a user of simglucose
receives. In the titrated arm the same subjects are dosed from settings weakened until each
subject's own record clears the consensus hypoglycaemia thresholds, which is closer to what a
clinician would prescribe. The four layers were then applied individually to the titrated arm and
together, and the two arms carrying the behaviour layer were additionally run with every meal
announced and with none announced. Meal announcement is a property of the simulated person, so
arms without the behaviour layer receive the exact carbohydrate content of every meal and cannot
carry that variation.

Outcomes are read from the sensor trace, since the real references are sensor traces. Time in
range is 70 to 180 mg/dL (3.9 to 10.0 mmol/L), time below range is reported at 70 mg/dL and at 54
mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L), and time above range at 180 mg/dL. Each is given as a median with a 95 per
cent percentile interval resampled over subjects, a mean with its standard deviation, and the
range across subjects. The median and the mean are both reported because the trials report means
and a third of subjects record no time below 54 mg/dL, where the two statistics differ by a
factor of three.

A simulated arm is recorded as differing from its reference when the reference median falls
outside the arm's interval. A difference expressed in units of the between-person spread of the
reference describes its size and does not establish that it exceeds the variation between cohorts
drawn from the same population.

Completion is reported for every arm. A run terminates when glucose leaves the range over which
the model is defined, terminating runs are those that departed furthest from target, and every
median is therefore conditioned on survival.

DCLP3 supplies 67 participants with per-participant outcomes on all six measures, and the Commons
77 users on the same six. CLOSE-IT publishes a mean and a standard deviation for time in range and
no participant-level data, so it supports no median, no spread and no test.

## Results

### The two reference points

Titration of the settings, holding the model unchanged, moved no outcome measurably on the DCLP3
cohort. Time in range was 90.8 per cent (95% CI 88.6 to 92.7) in the stock arm and 90.8 (89.0 to
92.7) after titration. It moved completion, from 81 of 84 subjects to 84 of 84, and removed the
three hypoglycaemic terminations the stock arm recorded. Time below 54 mg/dL was 0.3 per cent
(0.1 to 0.6) and 0.2 (0.1 to 0.5).

The infusion site layer accounts for most of the movement in the measures describing where glucose
sits. Applied alone it raised time above 180 mg/dL from 6.3 to 13.8 per cent and mean glucose from
128.7 to 138.9 mg/dL, against 14.9 and 141.7 for all four layers together.

The physiology layer accounts for the movement in variability, raising the coefficient of
variation from 25.5 to 31.8 per cent against a reference 33.4. This is the only arm on any measure
whose median cannot be distinguished from the DCLP3 participants.

The sensor layer moved no outcome measurably. Its function is to place compression lows and
measurement error in front of the algorithm rather than to alter the underlying glucose.

The behaviour layer applied alone reduced time below range, from 1.8 to 1.1 per cent below 70
mg/dL and from 0.2 to 0.1 below 54, because it supplies the rescue carbohydrate the model
otherwise lacks.

### Completion

| Arm | Completed | Terminations |
|---|---|---|
| stock | 81/84 | 3 hypoglycaemic |
| titrated | 84/84 | none |
| physiology | 55/84 | 28 hypoglycaemic, 1 hyperglycaemic |
| behaviour | 84/84 | none |
| sensor | 84/84 | none |
| site | 82/84 | 1 hypoglycaemic, 1 hyperglycaemic |

| all four, unannounced | 72/84 | 8 hypoglycaemic, 4 hyperglycaemic |

The physiology layer applied alone lost 29 of 84 subjects, 28 of them hypoglycaemically. It
carries the physiological variation without the behaviour layer that supplies rescue
carbohydrate, so a subject whose glucose falls has no means of recovery. Its outcome medians are
computed over the subjects that avoided a terminating low, and its agreement with the reference on
variability is conditioned on that selection.

## Discussion

The layers move the model in the direction of the real cohorts on the measures describing where
glucose sits, and close approximately a third of the distance to DCLP3 on time in range and on
mean glucose. They close the variability difference almost entirely. On the strongest of the three
pairings no configuration produced a cohort indistinguishable from the participants on time in
range, mean glucose or time above range, so a benchmark run here is run on a population that
remains about ten percentage points of time in range better controlled than the DCLP3 arm.

Two layers account for almost all of the effect and they act on different measures. The site layer
acts on hyperglycaemia and the physiology layer on variability. The sensor layer alters what the
algorithm observes rather than what the subject experiences, and its absence from these outcomes
is consistent with that design.

Time below 54 mg/dL is the exception to the pattern. The stock model agrees with the DCLP3
participants at 0.3 per cent against 0.2, and every layered arm exceeds them, reaching 0.8 per cent
with all four layers. The same measure carries the widest floor between disjoint cohorts drawn
from this population, at 1.06 to 3.65 spreads depending on the cell, and the largest excess in the
package's between-subject spread, at five to seven times the reference. Inferences about
hypoglycaemia drawn from this bench are the least supported of any reported here.

The settings a subject is dosed on are separable from the model and were separated here. Titration
changed no outcome measurably and changed completion, which is consistent with its purpose: it
removes the subjects that cannot be dosed safely at settings measured from their own physiology, a
quantity no person has.

Three limitations bound these results. Completion differs between arms by up to 29 subjects, so
the arms do not describe the same set of people and the medians of the worst-completing arms are
conditioned on the subjects that survived. The CLOSE-IT reference is a published summary without
participant-level data, so no test was possible against it and its cohort was matched on drawn
rather than recorded ages. And the reference populations differ in what they contain: DCLP3
randomised its participants, the Commons is self-selected among people who chose an open-source
system, and agreement with one is not agreement with the other.
