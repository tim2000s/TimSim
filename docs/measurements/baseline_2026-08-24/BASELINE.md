# Proximity to real closed-loop cohorts under the simulator as configured, against stock UVA/Padova S2008

## Summary

The configured simulator sits closer to all three real populations than the stock model does on
every measure describing where glucose sits, and further from them on both hypoglycaemia measures.
How much closer depends on which real population is asked, by more than the difference between the
algorithms being run: 4.8 percentage points of time in range against a self-selected group of
people who built their own closed loop, 12.0 against a randomised trial arm, and 16.3 against a
randomised trial of the same algorithm family as the first. The reference belongs in any statement
of how far this simulator sits from real people, because on its own the number is not one thing.

## Introduction

The UVA/Padova S2008 model reproduces the glucoregulatory physiology of type 1 diabetes and omits
most of what surrounds it. It has no infusion set that stops absorbing, no sensor error beyond
quantisation, no person who mistimes a meal or misjudges its carbohydrate, and no day-to-day
movement in insulin sensitivity. Its subjects are also dosed from settings derived from their own
physiology, a quantity no person has. A cohort run under a closed loop on that basis spends more
time in the target range than the people such a loop is prescribed to.

This package changes both parts. It adds four layers to the simulated person, covering
physiological variation, behaviour, sensor error and infusion site failure, and it titrates each
subject's settings until that subject's own record clears the consensus hypoglycaemia thresholds.
What follows reports how far the configured simulator sits from three real closed-loop populations
compared with the stock model, at each of three meal-announcement conditions.

## Methods

The population holds 600 subjects generated by resampling the thirty published UVA/Padova subjects
within age class, of which 330 have been through titration and 306 clear the consensus floors.
Cohorts were drawn from it to match each real reference on age and recorded total daily insulin,
giving 86 titrated subjects against the closed-loop arm of DCLP3, 70 against the OpenAPS Data
Commons and 73 against CLOSE-IT. Each cohort was run under the algorithm its reference population
used, a Control-IQ stand-in for DCLP3 and oref0 SMB for the other two, so a simulated cohort is
never compared with participants using a different system.

Four arms were run per pairing, 28 days each, 916 runs in total. The stock arm is the model as
simglucose supplies it: no layers, and each subject dosed from the settings its own physiology
implies, with the algorithm told the true carbohydrate content of every meal at the minute it is
eaten. The remaining three carry all four layers on titrated settings and differ in what the
algorithm is told about meals. Announced declares every meal, default declares 80 per cent of main
meals and 35 per cent of snacks, and unannounced declares none. In all three the declared amount
carries a 25 per cent coefficient of variation against the amount eaten, and the carbohydrate
reaches the gut ten minutes after the person is recorded as eating, because the estimating and the
gastric delay are both part of what the layers add.

The meal schedule is grounded on when real people log carbohydrate. 292,140 entries from 147
Commons users were placed at their local hour through the timezone each user's own profile
records, and the template reproduces the measured share of grams in every window of the day to
within 0.7 percentage points, at 5.2 eating occasions a day against a logged 5.56. Total intake is
held at 192 g/day expected at 70 kg rather than the logged median of 162, because logged grams
under-state eaten grams and the under-statement cannot be placed by hour. The schedule is part of
the simulated person rather than a layer, so the stock arm eats the same meals.

Sensor noise is drawn per subject and held for the run, from a lognormal with a log standard
deviation of 0.33 about a jitter of 1.93 mg/dL. Real users differ from one another in how noisy
their sensor traces are, and a single noise level applied to every subject removes a source of
between-person spread that the reference populations carry.

Outcomes are read from the sensor trace, since the real references are sensor traces. Time in range
is 70 to 180 mg/dL (3.9 to 10.0 mmol/L), time below range is reported at 70 mg/dL and at 54 mg/dL
(3.0 mmol/L), and time above range at 180 mg/dL. Each is given as a median with a 95 per cent
percentile interval resampled over subjects. Both the median and the mean are reported because the
trials report means and many subjects record no time below 54 mg/dL, where the two statistics
differ severalfold.

An arm is recorded as differing from its reference when the reference median falls outside the
arm's interval. A difference expressed in units of the between-person spread of the reference
describes its size and does not establish that it exceeds the variation between cohorts drawn from
the same population.

Completion is reported for every arm. A run terminates when glucose leaves the range over which the
model is defined, terminating runs are those that departed furthest from target, and every median
is therefore conditioned on survival.

DCLP3 supplies 67 participants with per-participant outcomes on all six measures and the Commons 77
users on the same six. CLOSE-IT publishes a mean and a standard deviation for time in range in each
of its two arms and no participant-level data, so it supports no median, no spread and no test.

This reference supports no test and publishes time in range alone, as a mean per arm: 69 ± 13 per

The contrast the trial was built to measure agrees. Withdrawing meal announcement costs the
simulated cohort 1.3 percentage points of time in range on the means, against a published adjusted

effect is inside the trial's own interval, and those are separate properties.

### Which real population is being asked

The three references disagree about how far this simulator sits from real people, and the
disagreement is larger than anything the simulator does. Running both cohorts under both
controllers separates the possible causes, paired per subject over those completing both arms
(`examples/controller_crosscheck.py`).

| Cohort | Control-IQ stand-in | oref0 SMB | Paired difference, 95 per cent interval | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DCLP3, 86 subjects | 83.3 | 85.9 | -2.21 (-3.51 to -1.40) | 72 |
| Commons, 70 subjects | 82.1 | 84.9 | -2.31 (-4.40 to -1.59) | 55 |

The two simulated cohorts behave almost alike, 1.2 percentage points apart under the stand-in and
1.0 under oref0. The two real populations do not: a median time in range of 71.3 for the trial arm
and 80.1 for the Commons users, 8.8 apart. Holding the algorithm fixed makes the same point
without the simulator in it. The Commons users and the CLOSE-IT participants both ran an
oref-derived system, and their times in range are 80.1 and 69.0 per cent. Two randomised
populations on different algorithms sit within 2.3 points of each other, and a self-selected
population on the same algorithm as one of them sits 11 points above both.

The stand-in is the less favourable of the two controllers rather than the more favourable one,
spending 3.5 percentage points more time above 180 mg/dL than oref0 on the same subjects. Running
the DCLP3 cohort under the shipped algorithm widens its distance from the trial from 12.0
percentage points to 14.6, so none of the gap is explained by the Control-IQ arm being a shape
rather than the real thing.

Against the Commons the simulated best-controlled tenth already carries slightly more time above
180 than its real counterpart, and the difference then grows at every percentile above it. The
shortfall is an absent group of subjects who do badly rather than a uniform displacement, and the
same shape holds against DCLP3 with a displacement underneath it.

The displacement has a clock. By hour of day, the deficit in mean glucose against DCLP3 spans 0.4
to 25.7 mg/dL, with 36 per cent of the whole day's deficit in the six hours from 21:00 to 03:00:
the participants' evening glucose persists into the night at 150 to 167 mg/dL while the simulated
cohort returns to near its fasting level, and their inter-meal troughs ratchet upward through the
day while the simulated cohort's do not (`docs/measurements/day_profile.json`).

## Discussion

The configured simulator sits closer to all three real populations than the stock model does on
every measure describing where glucose sits. Against DCLP3 at the default announcement condition
it halves the difference in time in range and in mean glucose and reduces the difference in
variability from 12.0 percentage points to 3.4. Against the Commons it agrees on variability at
every announcement condition and on mean glucose at two of the three.

How large the residual is depends on the reference and not on the simulator. The same configured
cohorts sit 4.8 percentage points of time in range above the Commons users, 12.0 above the DCLP3
arm and 16.3 above the CLOSE-IT participants. The crosscheck attributes 2.6 points of that range
to the choice of controller and 1.2 to the choice of cohort; what is left is the people. A
randomised trial arm and a group who chose to build their own closed loop differ by 11 percentage
points while running the same family of algorithm, and this simulator resembles the second. Any
algorithm benchmarked on this configuration should be assumed to score better than it would in a
randomised trial population, and the size of that flattery depends on which population the claim
is about.

Six candidate explanations of the residual have been tested against measurement and none survived;
the ledger with criteria and results is `docs/DESIGN_ALIGNMENT.md`. Meal announcement, survivor
bias, delivered insulin, the correction factor, per-subject engagement and the intake clock are
all refuted, the last three because the loop and the model absorb input-side perturbations: a
2.7-fold weakening of the correction factor moved time in range by 0.3 points, and regrounding the
meal template onto the measured intake clock of 147 real users closed 9 per cent of the night
deficit where a third was required. The excursion decomposition locates what remains: the
simulated descent from a peak runs at 62.7 mg/dL per hour against a real 59.5, so recoveries are
right, and the shortfall is too few excursions with peaks too low, on a trace whose evening
glucose does not persist into the night as the participants' does.

Hypoglycaemia moved toward the participants under the current template and remains the least
supported part of the bench. The default arm's 0.5 per cent below 54 mg/dL is distinguishable from
the reference 0.2, its between-subject spread is 4.38 times the reference against DCLP3, and the
measure carried the widest variation between disjoint cohorts when that was last measured, under a
settings store four revisions old. Hypoglycaemia inferences from this bench should carry those
qualifications.

Four limitations bound these results. Completion differs between the stock and configured arms by
up to seven subjects, so they do not describe the same set of people, and the configured medians
are conditioned on a group that excludes more of the subjects reaching a terminating low. The
CLOSE-IT reference is a published summary without participant-level data, so no test was possible
against it and its cohort was matched on drawn rather than recorded ages; its results are a
conference presentation rather than a paper, which is weaker provenance than any other figure
here. The three reference populations differ in kind, so agreement with one is not agreement with
another. And the Control-IQ arm is a shape built from a published description rather than the
shipped algorithm, which the crosscheck shows costs 2.2 percentage points of time in range against
oref0 on the same subjects.
